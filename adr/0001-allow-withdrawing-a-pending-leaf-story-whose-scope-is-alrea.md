# ADR-0001: Allow withdrawing a pending leaf story whose scope is already owned

**Status:** Accepted
**Context:** G-C2 caps this goal at two stories. The planner emitted exactly two — S1 (cherry-pick, `dependsOn: []`) and S2 (wiring, `dependsOn: ["S1"]`) — but the graph grew to four. S2 added S7 on a premise that proved wrong, then found it could not take S7 back: `validateRuntimeReplanMutation` refused every replacement-free removal with `destructive_removal`, so the graph could only ever grow. Challenge challenge-133569c8-434f-4a3d-baa1-5037cf7d465c recorded the violation as unrecoverable.

The blanket refusal was broader than the hazard it was written for. Its stated fear, echoed at `execution/conductor.ts:717`, is a pure "skip this failed story" replan silently leaving acceptance criteria undone. That case was already closed somewhere else: `collective-board.ts:2485-2501` builds `immutableStoryIds` from `completed`, `failed`, `leases`, `pendingQuality`, `pendingRecovery`, `recoveryContext` and `pendingCleanup`, and `runtime-replan.ts` refuses any mutation touching them as `immutable_story`. A failed or running story is therefore unremovable regardless. Two further precise checks already ran on the candidate graph: one refusing a candidate where a GoalContract invariant loses its last story owner, another for architecture obligations. The blanket guard returned before any of them, so its only live effect was to trap a pending, never-started story whose scope was provably already covered.
**Decision:** Keep removal fail-closed by default, but let the precise checks arbitrate. `validateCandidateGraph` now runs first; a replacement-free removal is admitted only when it is provably scope-preserving, and is otherwise still rejected as `destructive_removal`. A withdrawal qualifies when the PRD is governed (an ungoverned PRD derives an empty contract, which makes the coverage proof vacuous rather than passing, so the original blanket refusal stands), every removed story is a leaf, and the coverage checks pass. Leaf-ness is what preserves the original comment's point that a dependency-only rewire does not replace deleted scope: dropping a story out of the middle of a chain and rewiring its dependents around the hole is still refused.

Only `src/runtime/runtime-replan.ts` changes. `src/runtime-graph/legacy-replan.ts` keeps its own unconditional refusal, so the Conductor's automatic drain at `conductor.ts:717` is untouched.
**Consequences:** A proposer can retract its own mistaken, not-yet-started addition without inventing replacement work, so a constraint like G-C2 stops being one-way. Withdrawal never reaches a completed, failed, running or leased story, and never removes the last owner of an invariant or obligation — those refusals are unchanged and now carry their own precise rejection codes (`immutable_story`, `invalid_proposal`) instead of being masked by `destructive_removal`.

What the rule does not prove is that a withdrawn story's bespoke acceptance text was redundant; it proves only that the contract-level scope survives. That judgment stays with the proposer and is recorded in the replan reason and the audit event. This does not retroactively shrink the current run, whose orchestrator is the installed build rather than this source: S7 remains in that graph. It removes the trap for every later run.
